Tutorial 12 - Small worlds (centimetre-scale navigation)

Applies to LCM Nav3D 3.6.0 · Unreal Engine 5.2 to 5.8 · Updated 24 October 2026

In short: the plugin voxelizes in Unreal units (1 uu = 1 cm). A 120 × 70 × 40 cm aquarium with 5 cm cave openings navigates at a 1 cm leaf with either of two setups: at 100:1 on any release, or at 1:1 with the World Scale profile set to Small. Both were tested in the same tank.

Two rules to know first

  1. Set half the leaf you want. The octree halves a half-extent until it drops below Min Voxel Size, so the finest leaf lands at 2-4× the value. For a 1 cm leaf set 0.5.
  2. Make the world edge a power-of-two multiple of the leaf. World Extent is a cube half-extent centred on the world origin (the manager actor's location is ignored). With World Extent 64 and Min Voxel Size 0.5 the halving chain lands exactly on 1 cm; with 60 it lands on 0.94.

Option A - build at 100:1 (works on every release)

Setting Real In Unreal at 100:1
Tank 120 × 70 × 40 cm 12000 × 7000 × 4000 uu
Manager: World Extent - 6400 (finite)
Manager: Min Voxel Size - 50
Clearance Padding fish half-width half-width × 100
Fish speed 6 cm/s 600 uu/s (the shipped default)

In testing, agents found and used a 5 cm cave mouth, a blocked cave was correctly reported as unreachable, and an agent crossed the tank in 3 s.

Option B - build at 1:1 with World Scale = Small

Set World Scale to Small, World Extent to 64, Min Voxel Size to 0.5. Clearance Padding is derived for you (half the leaf); the start-rescue distances and the large-node cost threshold scale with the leaf as well. The Details panel will tell you that the world is only 128 leaves across. That is normal for a small world. The line-of-sight sampler, the neighbour probe and the route executor's sampling floors follow the leaf on their own, on every profile.

Agents follow too. The Fly To task (BT and StateTree) and the flight movement component carry an Agent Scale that defaults to 0 = follow the manager's World Scale, so under Small a fish authored with the shipped drone values (600 cm/s, 100 cm acceptance, 120 cm agent size) flies at 6 cm/s with a 1 cm acceptance and a 1.2 cm agent size, along the same path shape, in the same time. You still have to scale the pawn actor itself (mesh and collision) to match, because the plugin only scales the motion. Set Agent Scale explicitly on a task when an agent should not follow the world (a large creature in a small world, or the reverse).

There is one engine limit for very small bodies. Unreal's swept movement pulls every blocking hit back by a fixed 0.1-1 cm, and it treats gaps inside the physics contact offset (2 cm minimum) as an overlap. So a body with collision of a centimetre or less touches walls differently from the same body at metre scale. We tested this with the bird pawn at agent scale 0.01. Its flight matched the 100:1 flight exactly until it first touched a wall (and in this tank it arrived sooner after that). Bodies that stay clear of walls, like the navigator drones, match exactly the whole way. If wall-hugging behaviour must match a metre-scale reference exactly, build at 10:1 (agent scale 0.1), where the engine tolerances are still small against the body.

Stones with caves: use the collision

The plugin decides what is solid from collision, not from the visible mesh. Set stone meshes to Use Complex Collision As Simple. A convex hull or box that covers a cave mouth makes the cave solid for the plugin, however small the leaf is. (In testing, the same tank with the stones' collision removed routed straight through them. With complex-as-simple collision the result was exactly the same as the reference.)

Props that must not be solid

Grass, backdrops and other dressing a body should pass through need the collision profile NoCollision, not only Collision Enabled = No Collision: a component that keeps the BlockAll profile gets its collision back when the map loads, and is then voxelized like stone. (In one test, seagrass saved that way blocked a 4 cm body from two of the demo tank's goals.)

Mass crowds at small scale

A Mass trait carries no reference-agent constants, so author its values in world centimetres: the demo school uses a 2 cm Agent Radius, 25 cm/s Max Speed and a 6 cm Arrive Radius. The flow field's own centimetre constants (the arrival stop, the goal cell, the obstacle skin and the slide's push cap) follow the World Scale automatically. The LCM Nav3D ISM Render trait has Mesh Rotation and Mesh Offset for a mesh not modelled facing +X; a fish modelled nose-along +Y needs Yaw -90.

The demo tanks

Demo/BehaviourTree/PreBaked_FiniteMaps/FishTank_BT, Demo/StateTree/.../FishTank_ST and Demo/MassEntity/.../FishTank_MassEntity are a 120 x 70 x 40 cm aquarium built this way (option B, a 1 cm leaf). Ten 8 cm fish on the Fish archetype roam through a rock arch and round a predator's lair their Prey filter prices x10; in the Mass tank a school of 40 follows a leader fish and parts round a predator that carries its own Territory.

Your own aquarium: the checklist

The demo tanks already have all of this set up. In your own tank, check each item.

  1. Agent Scale matches the fish. Every Flight Config value describes a ~1 m agent. Set the Flight Movement component's Agent Scale to the fish's length / 100 (0.08 for an 8 cm fish), and the same on its Fly To nodes - or set the navigation manager's World Scale instead. If you leave it at 1, an 8 cm fish swims at 280 cm/s like a metre-sized agent, with a turning circle of about 1.4 m. It then circles its targets and crashes into the rest of the school. The component says so in the Output Log, once per Blueprint, with the value to set.
  2. A Water Volume round the water. Add a Physics Volume, tick Water Volume, and size it to the water. A Fish-archetype body inside it never leaves it, and its top face is a navigation boundary, so routes stay under the surface. Without one, the plugin doesn't know where the water ends. A route between two points can then go through the air above a rock or over the glass, and a fish can swim out of the tank. (The demo tanks have one; r.LcmNav.WaterSurfaceIsBoundary, r.LcmNav.Flight.FishStayInWater. A water volume wider than 200 m - a sea - gets no surface boundary, r.LcmNav.WaterSurfaceMaxSizeCm: its sheet would split the octree along the whole surface.)
  3. Glass that is solid to the octree. The panes need collision that blocks WorldStatic and at least two leaves of thickness (2 x Min Voxel Size); a pane one leaf thick can voxelize open.
  4. Spacing is automatic. A Fish-archetype pawn keeps its personal space (repulsion, collision anticipation, contact push) even with Schooling off; tick Schooling > Enabled for the full school (heading matching, closing up, leadership, predator escape) and set Body Length Cm to the fish's length and Body Width BL to its width over its length (0.25 for a slim fish, ~0.4 for a deep-bodied one such as a clownfish). Every flight body is a neighbour to the others; no Navigation Invoker is needed on the fish.
  5. Arrival. A fish slows until its turning circle fits the way onto its target (r.LcmNav.Flight.TurnAwareArrival), so it does not orbit a goal it came at from the side. Raise the Fly To's Acceptance Radius if the fish should settle further out.

What to expect at small scale

  • Memory grows quickly as the leaf gets smaller. In a world only a few dozen leaves across, every point is close to geometry when measured in leaves, so the octree can't use large nodes anywhere. In the test tank, the node count grew 7.3-7.9× each time the leaf size was halved. The voxel budget (64 M cells per volume) still applies.
  • Unreachable goals are cheap. Free-space components are labelled at build time, so a goal behind sealed stone fails before any search runs.
  • Bakes land in your project. Bake to Asset writes under Project Settings → LCM Nav3D → Baked Navigation Path (default /Game/LcmNavigation/BakedData). If you point that at a plugin's content folder, the manager warns you when it bakes a map from outside that plugin, because the bake would then ship with the plugin.
  • Don't use infinite mode for small worlds. Its per-chunk budget forces Chunk Size ≤ 400 × Min Voxel Size; the panel says so if you try.
