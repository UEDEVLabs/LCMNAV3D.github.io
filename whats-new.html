What's new in LCM Nav3D 3.6.0

Applies to LCM Nav3D 3.6.0 · Unreal Engine 5.2 to 5.8 · Updated 22 November 2026

Release 3.6.0 (October 2026) supports Unreal Engine 5.2 to 5.8. Each engine version has its own build of the plugin, so install the one that matches your project. This release adds moving-obstacle prediction to Fly To, a self-check for GPU voxelization, better fish swimming and schooling, and a new Obstacle Gauntlet demo. It also has more than thirty bug fixes from a review of the whole plugin.

New

  • Fly To avoids moving obstacles. Fly To now predicts where each moving obstacle (any actor with an LCM Dynamic Obstacle component) will be, and times its pass. It learns the cycle of a gate or a piston, turns with a spinning blade, and slows down, waits for a gap or takes a detour. If something comes at it too fast, it dodges. It is on by default (Avoid Moving Obstacles, with Moving Obstacle Look-Ahead and Moving Obstacle Margin) in the Behavior Tree and StateTree Fly To. See Dynamic obstacles.
  • Obstacle Gauntlet demo. A 370 m hall with six zones: fast gates, crossing traffic, windmills and sweepers, pistons, head-on blocks and bouncers. It has 42 moving obstacles, all driven by Unreal's own movement components. In the Behavior Tree and StateTree versions twelve drones fly the whole hall, and in the Mass version a crowd of 200 follows a beacon through it. Open it from the demo menu.
  • GPU voxelization checks itself. With Voxelization Method set to Auto, each machine tests its GPU before building navigation on it. There is a capability check, a self-test through the real pipeline (run once per GPU, driver and build, then remembered), and spot checks while the game runs. If the GPU fails, the plugin voxelizes on the CPU for the rest of the session. The log says which check failed and why, and the manager's On Voxelization Changed event fires. Voxelization Method set to GPU stays on the GPU. See the Settings reference.

Improved

  • Better fish swimming. Fish hold their depth and climb by pointing their nose up instead of sliding up and down. They stay upright, school side by side and in layers, and turn away from walls straight away. The tail beats with the swimming speed (Tail Beat From Speed in the Flight Config), and a skeletal fish's swim animation plays at the same rate. See Flight and locomotion.
  • Drones and hunters. Drones no longer bob up and down over a perch or a landing pad. Engage holds a firing position until its target leaves it, flies to it in formation and leads a crossing target. Hunters remember what they lose sight of and search for it, and gliding birds land from where they are. See Behaviour nodes.

Fixes

  • GPU navigation on more graphics cards. On some GPUs and drivers (first reported on a laptop RTX 3060) the GPU-built navigation could come back incomplete and agents stood still. The GPU build now checks its own output word by word, rewrites anything that did not get written and rebuilds what it cannot repair. This applies to D3D12, D3D11 and Vulkan.
  • Water and triggers no longer count as walls. The agents' own checks treated a Water Volume or an overlap-only trigger as solid. In water this meant fish found no cover, an ROV found no viewpoint and turtles found no seabed. Agent, tactical, EQS and path-smoothing checks now count only collision that blocks, and cover, firing and vantage points are chosen inside the navigation volume.
  • Obstacles that jump back to the start of their track. When a moving obstacle reset to the start of its path (a loop that jumps back), the plugin read it as moving very fast. Its update was dropped and the space it had left stayed blocked. The plugin now handles the jump correctly.
  • No crash without a renderer. A dedicated server, or any run with -nullrhi, crashed on a streamed level because navigation data was sent to a GPU that wasn't there.
  • Thirty fixes from a code audit. A review of the whole plugin fixed thirty defects across Fly To, crowds, flight, streaming and replication, and the plugin now logs under its own category (LcmNavigationLog).

LCM Nav3D 3.5.0

This release added landing, hunting, hiding and investigating, and made the plugin much smaller. It has behaviour nodes for flying agents (Engage, Investigate, Flee, Scan Area, Perch, Land and Take Off) for Behavior Trees, StateTree and Mass crowds, six new demo levels, eight Feature Examples levels, fish that behave in your own aquarium, and the demo levels became a free download from inside the editor.

New

  • A QuickStart level inside LCM Nav3D. /LCMNav3D/QuickStart/QuickStart opens with nothing else installed: a flying agent planning over and under obstacles, agent size, a patrol, and paths queried from Blueprint. Each display shows what it is checking while it runs. Walk up to a display to start it; O shows the planned routes. See Installation.
  • Behaviour nodes: Engage, Investigate, Flee, Scan Area, Perch. Ready-made behaviours for flying agents, each as a Behavior Tree task, a StateTree task and a Mass crowd task. Engage Target finds a firing position and keeps line of sight; Investigate searches around a last-known position; Flee breaks line of sight and hides; Scan Area sweeps a volume; Perch lands on tagged ledges and pads. Agents in a crowd share the work: no two take the same perch, cover or vantage point. See Behaviour nodes.
  • Land and Take Off. Drones descend vertically, birds and fish glide in with a braking flare, and both can land on moving surfaces such as a rolling ship's deck or a moving lorry, or under a roof. New EQS generators and tests find landing sites for flying agents, and Engage can take its sightings from AI Perception.
  • Six new demo levels, and a new demo menu. Hunter Drone, Harbour, Sea Cliffs, Coral Reef, Warehouse and Sentry Drones, each in Behavior Tree, StateTree and Mass editions, from a new menu with previews. They run without Lumen, so they perform well in any project.
  • Feature Examples: eight levels, one feature per display. Walk-through levels in the style of Epic's Content Examples: navigation basics, flight and locomotion, movement nodes, behaviour nodes, tactical queries and EQS, cost areas, dynamic and streamed worlds, and Mass crowds. Each display starts as you walk up, says what to look at and which chapter to read, and checks itself (PASS / FAIL on screen). See Feature Examples.

Changed

  • The demo levels are a separate, free download. The demo levels and their art are no longer inside LCM Nav3D. They are now LCM Nav3D Samples, which you download from inside the editor (LCM Nav3D menu > Get the Demo Levels). One click downloads them, checks every file and installs them as the plugin Plugins/LCMNav3DSamples. Updates only download what changed, and Remove uninstalls them without touching LCM Nav3D.
  • Fish in your own aquarium. Fish inside a Water Volume never swim out of it, slow down so their turning circle fits the way onto their target instead of circling it, and keep their personal space even with Schooling off. The body size is measured from the fish's meshes, and the editor warns about a pawn whose root has no collision or blocks other pawns. See Small worlds.
  • Flight follows the level's gravity; per-bird settings. Flying agents use the gravity of the level (World Settings or a Physics Volume) instead of a fixed value. Max Pitch, Flare Pitch and Leg Friction are now set per bird or fish in its Flight Config. See Flight and locomotion.
  • Renamed functions. Find Path Sync, Register Area, Unregister Area and Auto Register replace their old names. Blueprints and levels update automatically; C++ that uses an old name still compiles, with a warning naming the new one.

Fixes

  • Fly To no longer aims through walls in a finite level. A drone, fish or ROV could aim at a point past a corner and fly into the wall, because the check that keeps the aim point in sight only ran in infinite worlds. It now runs in both. Birds and jets keep their previous aiming.
  • Agents follow Preferred Lanes. A route planned along a cheaper area was cut short by the agent flying a straight line past it. Agents now stay on a cheaper corridor the planner chose.
  • Listen server and client no longer share perches. Perch, cover and position claims are kept per world, so a listen server and its client in Play-in-Editor no longer block each other's perches.
  • No false landscape message in Standalone. A Standalone or -game run no longer shows "LANDSCAPE: PHYSICAL MATERIAL NEEDS TO BE REBUILT" for a landscape whose data is up to date.

LCM Nav3D 3.4.1

Release 3.4.1 supports Unreal Engine 5.2 to 5.8. Each engine version has its own build of the plugin, so install the one that matches your project.

New

  • Fish schooling. Turn on Schooling on a Mass crowd or on a pawn's Flight Movement component and it steers as a school: personal space, collision anticipation, heading matching, and turning away from rocks and glass before reaching them. A few informed fish lead the rest, a predator sets off a fountain escape that spreads through the school, and fish swim in bursts and glides. Distances are in body lengths, so it works at any scale. See Mass traits and Fish schooling on pawns.
  • Hold Avoidance and Contact Push for Mass crowds. Hold Avoidance Between Scans keeps separation on every frame, not every other one. Contact Push pushes apart bodies that overlap, including agents that have arrived, so a crowd no longer piles up on its goal.

Changed

  • City demo maps (Behavior Tree, StateTree and Mass) have new building facades, detailed trees and rooftop equipment. Collision and navigation are unchanged, and the maps use nothing outside the plugin.
  • FlyTo's wall-avoidance whiskers no longer take nearby agents for walls; agent avoidance handles them. Set r.LcmNav.WallAvoidIgnorePawns 0 for the old behaviour.

Fixes

  • Mass agents froze against rocks and walls. A crowd agent pushed off the flow field's covered space stopped and never moved again. It now steers back to the nearest covered point, and a still agent is still pushed out of solid.
  • Hitch when spawning agents at runtime. Agents spawned while the game runs were registered as dynamic obstacles, and every one of them cost a navigation rebuild. Navigating agents are no longer dynamic obstacles.

LCM Nav3D 3.4.0

Release 3.4.0 supports Unreal Engine 5.2 to 5.8. Each engine version has its own build of the plugin, so install the one that matches your project.

New

  • Cost areas and query filters. Mark volumes as storms, preferred lanes, toll gates or no-fly zones with stock NavArea classes, and every planner routes by them: FlyTo (Behavior Tree and StateTree), Fly To Location, Mass crowds, Find Path and the stock Move To. A stock Navigation Query Filter on an agent decides what each area costs that agent, or forbids it, so two agents read the same volume differently. Moving areas are supported. See Cost areas and query filters.
  • Stock Move To and EQS plan through LCM Nav3D. The navigation proxy now registers for a real Supported Agent, and reports whether it did. See Stock Move To and EQS.
  • Fly To Location, a Blueprint node that flies a pawn with no Behavior Tree, StateTree or AI Controller. See Fly To Location.
  • Find Path Detailed tells a complete route from one that stops short of the goal in a streamed world, and the query option Require Complete Path makes a partial route fail instead.
  • FlyTo Timeout Seconds (Behavior Tree and StateTree): report Failed after a set time in flight.
  • Stay Running After Resolve on Find Cover, Chunk Prewarm and Streaming Aware (Find Actor By Tag and Pick Random Goal already had it), so a StateTree task that finishes at once no longer ends the FlyTo beside it.
  • New demo maps, on the demo menu in Behavior Tree, StateTree and Mass versions: Cost Areas and Cost Areas, Streamed (six labelled stations), and Fish Tank (a 120 cm aquarium navigated at 1 cm).

Changed

  • The plugin sets mass.FullyParallel 0 at startup. It avoids a UE 5.2 Mass phase-order ensure that froze the editor for seconds, and it was also faster at 10,000 agents. It never overrides a value you set; to opt out, use r.LcmNav.Mass.SerialPhases 0.
  • A Mass crowd's first flow field is built off the game thread, so a crowd spawning into a streamed world no longer costs one long frame.
  • A FlyTo with no route to its goal logs a Warning, not an Error.
  • Editor: every LCM Nav3D actor and component has its own icon in the Outliner and the Place Actors panel, and a What's New window opens once after each update, with buttons that open the new demo maps and guides. Reopen it from the Nav3D toolbar menu.
  • The demo HUD shows the plugin's current version.

Fixes

  • Packaged sample project: a packaged build started on an empty screen, because the demo menu and its maps were not cooked. The menu and every demo map are now included.
  • Vertical jitter with the Context-Based and Linear avoidance styles.
  • Stock Move To never reached LCM Nav3D, although the log said the proxy was registered.
  • Compiler warnings: the plugin builds with no warnings on every supported engine version.

LCM Nav3D 3.3.0

Release 3.3.0 supports Unreal Engine 5.2 to 5.8. Each engine version has its own build of the plugin, so install the one that matches your project.

New

  • A rebuilt agent task library for Behavior Tree, StateTree and Mass: Take Off, Hover, Land, Orbit, Look At, Turn In Place, Move To And Face, Follow Target (Escort), Patrol and Use Smart Object, plus Find Actor By Tag. Patrol now follows routed paths instead of straight lines. Property lists are in the API Reference, and Look At explains when to use the plugin's rotation tasks instead of the stock one.
  • Small worlds. Voxels down to 1 cm, a World Scale setting for tiny agents, and a Leaves padding mode. New tutorial: Small Worlds.
  • Streaming rebuilds. In an infinite world, when a level streams in after its area was already built, navigation for that area is rebuilt automatically. Three console variables control it; see Infinite World.

Fixes

  • UE 5.4 to 5.8: buildings and other triangle-mesh collision could be missing from the navigation voxels, so agents flew through them.
  • UE 5.6 to 5.8: GPU voxelization could return incomplete navigation data. Results now match the CPU voxelizer, and Voxelization Method Auto uses the GPU on every engine version.
  • UE 5.4 and 5.5: turning on Draw Debug Volumes crashed the engine.
  • All versions: simple (convex) collision was missing from GPU voxelization.
  • UE 5.7: the standalone game could crash on World Partition demo maps with landscapes.
  • Fly To: a move that ends early now clears its guidance, and failure cooldown and final-approach settling are more reliable.
  • Crowds: agents no longer slip through walls at chunk borders, and open-world crowds no longer get stuck on stale route data.
  • Demo maps: Dynamic Obstacles and Mixed Indoor/Outdoor fixed.

Documentation and licence

  • The manual, the quick start and every chapter are updated for 3.3.0, including the new task library.
  • The EULA is updated to version 3.3.0.
